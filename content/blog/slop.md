While it's hard to pin down an exact definition for slop, I would start with these properties: Low effort, and disingenuous. 

Works such as [Balloon Dog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balloon_Dog) by [Jeff Koons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Koons) by can be (arguably) disingenuous, but by virtue of the effort involved, escapes the Slop label to land as [Kitsch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitsch). 

Then you have your low-effort, genuine creations. This covers everything from everyday artistic endeavors such as my own homemade wall-art, to So-Bad-It's-Good cult classics like [The Room](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Room). The creators intentions really do matter, it seems.

Additionally, Slop only appears when there is incentive, monetary or otherwise. The blurry photo you snapped at that concert (that you're definitely doing to look at later!) is both low effort and not particularly genuine, but also isn't Slop. Perhaps you gain a bit of social clout by posting on your socials, but this isn't enough of an inventive to gain the attention of the Slop Machine.
